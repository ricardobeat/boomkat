# Widened function fragments, 2026-10-31

On rat `c74029b2`, the dynamically generated zlib function `a1` has 37,098 bytecode words and 518 VM registers. Its attempted region begins at prefix PC 31045. The captured bytecode in `../capture-dispatch-2026-10-31/a1-region-31045-bytecode.txt` starts with widened `LDINT r484`, `BOR`, `EQ`, and a conditional; it then reads globals and integer typed arrays. A prototype decodes both narrow and widened operands, addresses the original frame slots, uses the original prefix for refusal, and exits backwards branches to the interpreter for polling. It has guarded integer arithmetic, branches, bindings and integer typed-array accesses; unsupported operations exit. It is a bounded baseline experiment, not the shared cyclic CFG/SSA optimizer in plan 113.

ARM64 disassembly in `a1-wide-native-head.asm` shows the first r484 frame load and store at offset 0xf20 (484 * 8). The standalone zlib diagnostic installed this region and showed 169,358 entries. A block-local fact experiment reduced the body from 33,364 to 20,936 bytes, but permitting entries only at those fact boundaries reduced entries to 1,535; five alternating zlib mini medians were 3,718 ms control, 3,692 ms simple WIDE, 3,733 ms facts. Restoring entry boundaries after guarded binding and typed accesses improved the facts variant to 3,706 ms, still behind the simple version at 3,689 ms in a separate five-pair comparison. This variant was removed.

Sharing exit stubs for guards that resume the same bytecode PC reduced the same native fragment from 33,364 to 27,484 bytes while retaining 169,358 entries. All guards for an instruction exit before its effects; the shared stub only returns its original PC. Five alternating zlib mini medians were 3,710 to 3,679 ms; a seven-pair reversed-order confirmation was 3,707 to 3,689 ms. These are narrow timings, not full-suite scores. The shared disassembly is in `a1-wide-shared-native-head.asm`.

The first full-suite candidate did not admit the fragment: the 2 MiB function cap had already been exhausted even though a 4 MiB total including the loop reserve remained. A full diagnostic explicitly recorded the `a1` attempt at PC 31045 with 2,358,872 bytes already used and rejection `code budget or region bounds`; its `a1` interpreter counters were 3,000,373,671 threaded and 365,642,486 switch operations. Three alternating uninstrumented full pairs for that candidate had median aggregate 6,551 to 6,542; median zlib 3,377 to 3,403. That aggregate does not establish a speedup from a fragment that was not admitted.

A separate admission experiment permits WIDE regions reached from backedges to compete for a bounded 128 KiB share of the existing loop reserve, at most 64 KiB per fragment. The full diagnostic installed `a1`, entered it 1,725,725 times, and reported its major exits at JUMP PCs 31116 and 31159; final total executable bytes were 2,406,624 of 4,194,304. The full diagnostic score is instrumented and is not a release score. Three alternating uninstrumented full pairs for this admission experiment without shared stubs had median aggregate 6,552 to 6,582 and zlib 3,410 to 3,407. Other suite scores varied substantially; the aggregate movement is not evidence of a causal speedup from this fragment. Raw pairs and selected diagnostic lines are retained alongside this report.

The implementation checks guard exits, high registers, both arms of forward branches, integer typed-array reads/writes and a typed write followed by a later last-owned-string refusal in the embedding suite. ARM64 AddressSanitizer and x86-64 embedding runs passed for the shared-stub version. A separate JIT correctness issue was found: cache allocation may fail, and offsetting a null cache pointer for a nonzero region makes it appear nonnull. Guard compilation while either cache array is missing, and do not suppress a future retry at the native-entry gateway; the embedding checks exercise both arrays missing and each array missing independently.

Three alternating uninstrumented full pairs for the shared-stub candidate gave aggregate scores 6,525/6,567/6,550 versus 6,568/6,588/6,585 (medians 6,550 to 6,585), and zlib 3,404/3,409/3,399 versus 3,380/3,392/3,568 (medians 3,404 to 3,392). The aggregate difference is mostly unrelated workload variability, and zlib does not show a reliable win despite proven native entries. On the evidence, the separate baseline lowering and reserve policy were not retained; the patch and disassembly are saved for the shared decoded/SSA implementation. The cache-allocation correctness fix is independent and retained. The 50,000 target remains open.
